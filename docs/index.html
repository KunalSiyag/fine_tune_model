Sixteen unseen briefs

Each request below is one sentence a small-business owner might type. They were written before the model saw them, and nothing in the pipeline was tuned on them. Every result is shown, including the ones that didn't work.

one-liner→Gemma 4 writes the spec→ Qwen3-8B + LoRA writes 5 drafts→headless Chrome QA→ Gemma fixes JS errors→Gemma judges the first screen

All businesses, names, prices and contact details on these pages are fictional and were written by the models. Links, forms and booking widgets are demos.

How a site is chosen. QA renders every draft at 1440, 820 and 390 px and fails it on any JavaScript error, failed request, horizontal overflow, a page that vanishes under reduced motion, or a blank first screen. Gemma 4 then scores the first screen of each passing draft from 1 to 10 and the highest score is kept. A score under 6 is marked weak.

Draft labels. k1–k5 are the first five drafts, k6 and up were requested because the first pick looked weak, and an "r" suffix means Gemma repaired that draft's JavaScript.

What passing QA doesn't prove. The checks catch errors, overflow and blank screens, not whether every feature works. Several booking forms and calculators on passing pages do nothing when used, and some copy has factual or language mistakes, such as wrong answers in the Spanish level quiz.

Edits for publishing. Every live site was read in full before it went up. Phone numbers are replaced with zeros because an invented number can still belong to someone, one street address is removed, and one page's white-noise button is turned down. Nothing else was changed.
